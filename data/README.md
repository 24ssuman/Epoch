# Offline scientific data

`manifest.json` records installed-source URLs, local SHA-256 checksums, byte sizes, licenses/attribution and installation times. Complete kernel files are retained. Their temporary ranged-download shards under `tmp/downloads/` may be removed after assembly and checksum verification.

- kernels/: JPL DE440/DE441, GM constants and 16 asteroid J2000 states/source responses.
- catalogs/: HYG/Hipparcos, measured JMDC diameters, bright-star galpy trajectories and resumable checkpoints scoped to 15,000 BCE–5,000 CE; optional Gaia UWS job/partial download.
- comets/: individually bounded cached JPL apparitions and original responses.
- jobs/: numerical reconstruction checkpoints, accepted trajectory packs, validation reports, worker status and pause control.
- assets/: local illustrative Earth/Moon images.
- references/: original Horizons responses and parsed offline reference cases.

DE441 itself spans roughly 13,200 BCE–17,191 CE; it remains installed because its in-range portions supply planetary ephemerides, while API dates stay limited to 15,000 BCE–5,000 CE. All runtime data is local. Source links are attribution references, not background downloads. The installation plus retained generated/cache files is guarded against exceeding 10 decimal GB. No automatic deletion/eviction is performed.

- catalogs/sanskrit_star_names.json: Sanskrit star-name labels matched to catalog star IDs, with IAST, Devanagari, aliases, and identification notes. It also records English-only star mentions and the display policy; all listed stars lie within the common 5.76 magnitude limit (`catalog.STAR_LIMIT`). Names only; built and checked against `stars.json` by `scripts/install_sanskrit_names.py`. Source links and mapping limitations are summarized in the [frontend guide](../frontend/README.md#sanskrit-name-sources).
  `bodies` holds Sanskrit names for the Sun, Moon and planets with an evidence basis such as direct attestation, inference, conjecture, or modern convention.
- comets/photometry_overrides.json: sourced replacements for JPL M1/k1 when the JPL fit does not describe the near-perihelion apparition (currently Hale–Bopp 1997).

- references/crosscheck/: saved NASA/JPL Horizons responses and NASA GSFC eclipse-site pages (ΔT table, Five Millennium Canon of Solar Eclipses) used by scripts/crosscheck_nasa.py and tests/test_nasa_crosscheck.py. Source URLs and checksums are in manifest.json.
- catalogs/classical_star_names.json: 12 classical Sanskrit star names from Sūrya Siddhānta VIII and Bṛhat Saṃhitā 13.5–6, plus Marīci as an alternative for Alkaid. Each entry cites its source; generated by `scripts/install_classical_names.py`. Online text links are listed in the [frontend guide](../frontend/README.md#sanskrit-name-sources).
